## v4 (2026-11-08): a fish counter trained on the biologists' tally dots

**Why:** the motion detector's weakness is recall (it finds 14 of the 36 fish at 14:01). The biologists placed a dot on
every fish they counted in five gridded screenshots (36 + 102 on the survey video, 18 + 46 + 134 on T2-STAT2): point
labels are exactly what a density-counting model needs.

**Labels (verified, not assumed):** black dots are pure black (max channel < 30); at 22:00 the extraction reproduces the
handwritten number of all 12 grid cells (102), at 14:01 it returns the same 36 points as v1. Red T2 dots: 46 at 14 min
matches 30 + 9 + 7 handwritten. The clean survey-video frames were found by correlating each screenshot with every
frame in ±1.5 s (22:00 → 1320.32 s, margin 0.20; 14:01 → 841.27 s, margin 0.01, next to v2's 841.31 s). Red ink and grid
lines on the T2 screenshots are inpainted; the VLC bar and circled single organisms are excluded from training.

**Model:** frozen DINOv2-small (Apache-2.0) patch features + a 3-layer convolutional head (≈ 0.5 M parameters) predicting
fish per patch; optional motion channels (robust z-score against the ±8 s median background, the motion detector's own
signal, kept continuous). 1,500 steps, 4 views per image (flip, two brightness/contrast changes).

**Evaluation: leave one frame out.** Each row is a head trained WITHOUT that frame (mean of 2–3 seeds). Average error =
mean |prediction − biologists| / biologists over the five held-out frames; the configuration was chosen on that average.

**What this shows, honestly:**
* At **14:01** the counter trained without that frame gives **37.8 vs the biologists' 36**; the motion detector gave 14.
* At **22:00** (the faintest annotated frame) it gives **30.5 vs 102**: worse than the motion detector's 80. With one
  other survey-video frame to learn motion from, it cannot yet see those nearly invisible fish.
* So neither estimator is reliable alone. We do **not** combine them with a rule tuned on two frames. BlueSight reports
  the counter as a **second opinion** per subsample and flags subsamples where the two disagree (one > 1.5× the other
  and ≥ 5 fish apart) as **check first** in Count check, plus an optional heatmap of where the counter sees fish.
* On the protocol window (1:00–16:00) the shipped model (an ensemble of the 3 evaluated seeds) gives MeanCount 16.7
  against the motion detector's 20.7, and the two disagree on 11 of 15 subsamples. On the settling-sediment frame at
  1:00 the motion detector reports 109 and the counter 34.5; on murky frames (clarity < 0.25) the counter gives 1.7–11.
* Cost: 5.5 s per subsample frame on the M4 (≈ 80 s per 15-frame video, cached afterwards).

Selection note: the neighbouring-frame variant has the lower 5-frame average, so under a pure "average error" rule it
would win. We weigh the two survey-video frames first because that is the footage Coastal Action will analyse; this is
stated here rather than hidden.

* The final model (`models/density/head.pt`, 2 MB) is an ensemble of the 3 evaluated seeds, trained on all five frames. Every count-check correction adds

## What improved and why

1. **Detection: a modest, consistent gain, not a dramatic one.** Gentler, coarser contrast enhancement (CLAHE clip 2 on 4×4 tiles instead of clip 3 on 8×8) brings out the faint fish while amplifying sand texture and sunlight flicker less. On 13 consecutive video frames around the annotated 14:01 instant (the same frames for both versions): mean F1 0.420 → **0.461**, precision 0.49 → 0.58, recall 0.37 → 0.39. FINAL is better on 9 frames and worse on 4. That is **not statistically significant** (sign test p = 0.2668), and the central 5 frames were also used to choose the setting.
2. **Each fish keeps one ID more reliably.** Tracking at 2 frames per second only works if boxes are enlarged for matching. Enlarging small boxes to at least 200 px (was 120) and others less (×2.5, was ×3) cut split tracks from 6.64 to **1.30 per 100 tracks** on the tuning half. Merged-fish jumps went from 3.01 to 2.72.
3. **The biggest practical gains are in the review queue and the demo.** Review items fell from 50 to **37** (tuning half) and from 93 to **67** (demo half): less reviewer time for the same footage. In the demo window the held-out green crab is still review item #1 and still 'unfamiliar' (0.45). After a reviewer approves it, 5 tracks are recognised as crab, against 10 before. 3 of them fall inside the crab's walk (before: 2), so sand patches wrongly matched to the new species dropped from 8 to **2**. The demo half was never used to choose settings, so this is a held-out result.
4. **Counts at the two manually counted instants moved in opposite directions, so we don't claim a count improvement.** 14:01 (tuning half): 19 → 14 of 36. 22:00 (demo half, checked once afterwards): 61 → **80** of 102. A count at a single instant is dominated by which video frame happens to be sampled: one frame (33 ms) later, the detector found 17 instead of 27 objects at 14:01. BlueSight remains a pre-screen; it doesn't replace the manual count.
5. **Two tempting 'improvements' were rejected because they failed on real video.** Enhancing crops and using several prototypes per species both looked much better on the screenshot photos, but those photos separate known from held-out partly by background (pale water vs sandy seabed). On video tracks, enhanced crops dropped fish-vs-non-fish separation to chance, and k-means prototypes raised clear-water 'possibly new species' flags from 16.2% to 34.3%.
6. **Found and documented, not hidden:** ByteTrack's `lost_track_buffer` does nothing at 2 fps (a scaling quirk in `supervision`); the motion detector's background alternates between 15 and 16 frames depending on frame-time rounding (measured, no gain from fixing); the 14:01 ground truth belongs to 841.31 s, not 841.00 s (found by correlating the screenshot with the video); and OWLv2, base or large, finds none of these fish.
7. **Cost:** sampling stays at 2 fps (1 fps undercounts even with the same persistence time) and no bigger model was added. First-time processing measured 56.7 / 67.4 s of compute per video minute (tuning / demo half) vs 69.1 / 61.7 in v1. Run-to-run timing varies with other load on the laptop, so treat differences of ±15% as noise. Energy: 0.262 Wh per video minute (CodeCarbon estimate, CPU+RAM only).

## 5b. Video level: the held-out green crab in the demo window (`scripts/rehearse_demo.py`)

Demo window 1280–1355 s (21:20–22:35, demo half). A large European green crab, which is held out of the registry, walks along the pole from about 21:31 to 21:45 (checked by eye in the frames).

* **Before approval:** the crab's track is review item #1 (the most unfamiliar), status **unfamiliar**, best similarity 0.45 to the only registry species (21:32–21:48). It is **flagged as unfamiliar, not mislabelled.**
* **Approved** as 'european_green_crab' from the first item's 5 crops. The re-run took 38 s from cache.
* **After approval:** 5 tracks are recognised as european_green_crab. **3 of them fall inside the walking crab's time span**, including 2 track(s) nobody reviewed (21:29–21:42, 21:35–21:48), now recognised automatically. The other 2 are outside the crab's walk (sand or caustic patches that resemble the sandy background of the crab crops); 1 of them overlap 22:00, where the biologists circled a camouflaged crab, but their crops can't confirm it. 11 more tracks become 'probably european_green_crab?'.
* **Conclusion:** the flag-approve-recognise loop works, but a species approved from a single track has a loose prototype. On sandy footage it over-matches until reviewers confirm more examples (Existing species) and reject sand items. (v1 experiment: tighter crops, padding 0.6 → 0.15, improved separation only marginally, sand max 0.86 → 0.87 vs crab 0.83 → 0.85.)

## 6. Honest assessment

* **Detection is the weak link.** The fish are faint, translucent and about 50 px long at 1080p. The motion detector under-detects dense, slow schools and picks up some sunlight caustics on the sand. Many of those false positives are dropped as transient, but some persist and appear as 'uncertain' review items (a reviewer rejects them in one click).
* **Species identification is limited by the references.** The only fish references are screenshot crops of one unidentified school at another station (T2-STAT2). The 'known' species is therefore 'small schooling fish (species unconfirmed)'. BioCLIP zero-shot could not name the species reliably (see RESOURCES.md).
* The image-level flag test uses screenshot crops, which come from a different domain than live video crops. On video, the review queue is the safety net.
* Every result comes from one video and two manually counted frames. Results on other sites or seasons are unknown.
